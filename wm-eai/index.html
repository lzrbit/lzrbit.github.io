Abstract

Robots now plan and act with world models, learned predictors of how the world evolves under actions and interventions, and are trained and evaluated inside them. World model related surveys take the robot’s viewpoint, leave each use’s requirements implicit and overlook environments that predict and adapt to the robot; such assistance can be mistaken for robot competence. This survey makes four contributions. First, it places world models in one loop with two decision-makers, where an assignment rule separates agent-centric world models inside the robot, learned worlds in place of the world, and environment-enhanced world models that the environment queries for this robot. Second, it reviews the first two paradigms by query and use: in reported results, usefulness tracks action-faithful, closed-loop prediction more than visual realism, and learned evaluators are validated mainly by rank agreement on a few fixed policies. Third, its coded evidence map of environment-enhanced world models shows that environments predict the robot mainly with outcome critics and preview changes with physics engines and digital twins, rarely with learned world models. Fourth, it proposes a research agenda and an evaluation protocol separating robot learning from environmental assistance, even in learned worlds refit on the robot’s rollouts.

Three placements of a world model in one loop

The survey describes embodied systems as one loop with two decision-makers: the robot’s policy chooses actions , and the environment side’s rule chooses interventions , such as what the robot experiences, how its world is configured or what it is told. The same kind of predictor can sit inside the robot, in place of the world, or with an environment side that predicts this robot and adapts to it. Each placement owes its own guarantee.

Paradigm I: Agent-centric world models

Inside the robot

: the joint physical and task state; : the robot’s action. The robot’s own learning algorithm maintains the model.

Queries
Learning in imagination; planning, verifying and scoring; acting through the model.
Must guarantee
Respond faithfully to the robot’s actions, accurate where errors would change its decisions.
Example
V-JEPA 2-AC plans pick-and-place zero-shot on real Franka arms from image goals, a preprint reports.

Paradigm II: Learned worlds

In place of the world

The conditions are fixed or chosen by humans; the model does not condition on this robot.

Uses
Training inside the model; data engines; policy evaluation; scenario generation; twins and physics hybrids; rewards and judges.
Must guarantee
Reproduce the outcomes that training or evaluation relies on, such as how real policies rank.
Example
A Veo-based simulator evaluates Gemini Robotics policies; its preprint checks the predicted rankings against more than 1,600 real trials.

Paradigm III: Environment-enhanced world models

With the environment side, serving the robot

: robot variables such as competence; : embodiment; : the environment side’s history (its evidence about the robot).

Prediction questions
Choose experience (Q1); predict the robot (Q2); preview changes (Q3); share predictions (Q4).
Must guarantee
Predict conditioned on this robot and on interventions chosen for it.
Example
Sirius-Fleet’s monitor requests human help on real robots where a visual world model predicts failure under the fleet’s shared policy.

Is the model part of the robot?

It is an inner component of the robot’s own learning or control algorithm, for example a model the learner updates every iteration or whose uncertainty it penalizes; a model used only through its outputs and refit, if at all, in discrete rounds is not.

Does it stand in for the world ?

Its output serves as the robot’s experience or as the outcome by which the robot is judged.

Is it queried to choose an intervention for this robot?

The environment side uses its predictions to decide what to do for this robot.

Evidence on environment-enhanced world models

An environment-enhanced world model is a predictive model held by the environment side, a rule other than the robot’s own policy update; in the strict core, this model is a learned world model. It is queried to choose an intervention meant to improve this robot’s learning, safety or task success: what the robot experiences, how its world is configured or what it is told. The term denotes neither data or environment augmentation nor a world model enhanced with environment information.

The survey groups these uses by four prediction questions: Q1 choose experience (what should this robot experience next?), Q2 predict the robot (how will it perform, and where will it fail?), Q3 preview changes (what follows if the environment changes?) and Q4 share predictions (what should the environment tell this robot?).

Evidence map

The evidence map (Fig. 9 of the survey) places 67 coded papers by prediction question and by the type of model the environment side queries. They are the papers that answer one of the four questions among 122 papers on environment adaptation, each coded twice independently and adjudicated (searches of October 12 and 13, 2026): a coded set, not a census. Of the 67, 59 are Paradigm III entries; the other 8, Q1 entries at L0, do not condition on the robot and are reviewed as learned worlds. Only the first column passes the strict world model test.

The survey’s first finding concerns placement: learned world models are rare where the environment side predicts the robot (Q2) or previews changes (Q3). There they account for 2 of 21 and 3 of 18 entries, against 16 surrogates or critics and 11 engines, twins or analytic models. An updated search in which learned world models reach parity with surrogates in Q2, or with engines and twins in Q3, would refute this finding.
